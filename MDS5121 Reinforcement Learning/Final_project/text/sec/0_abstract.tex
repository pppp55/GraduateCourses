\begin{abstract}

Reinforcement learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for recommendation systems, as modern recommenders increasingly operate as sequential decision-making processes rather than static prediction models. Traditional supervised learning approaches are effective at optimizing short-term metrics such as click-through rate, but struggle to capture delayed feedback, user preference dynamics, and long-term value. Reinforcement learning (RL), with its ability to model interactions over time and optimize cumulative rewards, provides a principled framework for addressing these challenges.

In this survey, we provide a systematic review of reinforcement learning-based recommendation systems (RLRS). We first introduce the background and problem formulation of applying RL to recommendation scenarios. We then present a structured taxonomy that categorizes existing methods from the perspectives of learning paradigms, state representation, and exploration strategies. Representative works are discussed to highlight key technical innovations and practical insights. Furthermore, we analyze emerging research trends, including deep exploration and the integration of large language models (LLMs) with RL-based recommenders. Finally, we summarize open challenges and future research directions, aiming to provide a comprehensive reference for both academic researchers and industrial practitioners.
\end{abstract}
